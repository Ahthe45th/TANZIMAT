Short answer: yes. You can create virtual cards to pay online. Which one is best depends on where you live, whether you need multi-currency, and if this is for personal or business use. Below is a quick primer and practical options, with steps and country notes for Kenya.

# What a virtual card is

A virtual card is a card number (16 digits, expiry, CVV) issued digitally rather than on plastic. You can create single-use or reusable virtual cards, set spending limits, freeze or close them instantly, and usually track or lock them to specific merchants. They help reduce fraud and control subscription or ad spend. (General concept — see provider pages below.)

# Good options (with notes)

1. **M-PESA GlobalPay (Kenya)** — a Visa virtual card linked to your M-PESA wallet so you can pay international merchants from your mobile money balance. If you live in Kenya and use M-PESA, this is often the simplest route. ([Safaricom][1])

2. **Revolut** — supports creating virtual cards (including disposable ones) and is widely used for travel and online shopping. Revolut advertises the ability to create many virtual cards and manage security in the app. Availability depends on whether Revolut supports accounts for residents of your country. Check sign-up/verification rules in the app. ([Revolut][2])

3. **Wise (formerly TransferWise)** — offers multi-currency accounts and virtual Visa debit cards for customers in supported countries. Good when you need low-cost currency conversion and multi-currency spending. Availability depends on country; Wise publishes which countries can order cards. ([Wise][3])

4. **Payoneer** — widely used by freelancers and businesses. Payoneer can issue virtual Mastercard/Visa cards tied to your Payoneer balances. Good for business expenses and ad spends. Eligibility and card issuance can depend on account activity and country. ([Payoneer][4])

5. **Privacy.com and similar privacy-first services** — excellent for US customers because they let you create single-use and merchant-locked cards. Not available to non-US residents. If you are in Kenya, Privacy.com will not work for sign-up. ([Privacy][5])

# How to choose

* If you want simplest local solution in Kenya: try **M-PESA GlobalPay**. It uses money already in your M-PESA wallet and works on Visa merchants. ([Safaricom][1])
* If you need multi-currency and low FX fees: **Wise** is strong where available. ([Wise][3])
* If you run a business or get paid internationally: **Payoneer** or Wise Business are practical. ([Payoneer][4])
* If you value disposable single-use cards and you are a US resident: **Privacy.com** or similar. If you are not a US resident, these are usually not an option. ([Privacy][6])

# Typical steps to get a virtual card

1. Pick a provider and download its app or open an account on the website.
2. Verify your identity (photo ID, proof of address).
3. Fund the account or link a bank/card as required.
4. In the app, create a virtual card. Choose single-use or multi-use and set limits.
5. Use the card number, expiry, and CVV to pay online, or add it to Apple/Google Pay where supported.
6. Freeze or close the virtual card after use if you want extra safety.

# Things to watch out for

* Some merchant flows check billing address or country. Virtual cards from some providers may be rejected if the billing country does not match the merchant expectation.
* Fees and FX rates vary by provider. Always check the cost of loading the card and merchant currency conversion.
* Recurring subscriptions: single-use cards will break subscriptions; use a multi-use virtual card for recurring payments.
* Regulatory and availability limits: some services (Privacy.com, certain Revolut features) are restricted by country or residency. ([Privacy][6])
